Signal and power flow

One fused key-on 12V input feeds everything. The motor and pump side goes through the 20A blade fuse; the logic side through a reverse-polarity diode and an automotive 5V regulator.

The S13 motor's common brush sits at +12V all the time. It runs when the LO or HI brush is pulled to ground. K1 energized grounds the brush directly. K1 released hands the brush to the auto-stop contact, which keeps it grounded until the blades reach park, then switches it to +12V, which brakes the motor.

Because of that, the firmware never has to "stop at park". Releasing K1 always finishes the sweep. It also means a key-off mid-sweep parks the blades as soon as power returns.

Reading the stalk

The factory stalk has no electronics. Pin 17 is its common and goes to board ground, so each contact pulls its pin to 0V against a 2.2k pull-up. The INT ring is a 14–950Ω variable resistor from pin 19 to ground on the same kind of input, which reads 0.03–1.5V.

What you need

  • A UPDI programmer. The simplest is a ready-made "UPDI Friend" style USB programmer. Or build one from any USB-serial adapter (CH340, CP2102, FTDI): connect adapter RX straight to UPDI and adapter TX to UPDI through a 470Ω–1kΩ resistor. This is the "SerialUPDI" method.
  • 12V bench supply or battery on J1 (a 1A current limit is plenty for programming).
  • Arduino IDE 2 with the megaTinyCore board package.

Flash it

  1. Arduino IDE → File → Preferences → Additional boards manager URLs: http://drazzy.com/package_drazzy.com_index.json
  2. Tools → Board → Boards Manager, search megaTinyCore, install.
  3. Open firmware/s13_wiper/s13_wiper.ino.
  4. Tools: Board ATtiny3226/3216/1626/1616… (no Optiboot), Chip ATtiny1616, Clock 8 MHz internal, Programmer SerialUPDI - 230400 baud, Port = your adapter.
  5. Power the board with 12V on J1. Connect programmer UPDI to J5 pin 2 and GND to J5 pin 3. Leave J5 pin 1 (+5V) unconnected.
  6. Sketch → Upload Using Programmer (Ctrl+Shift+U). Expect "avrdude done" and the board LED stays off with the stalk in OFF.

Diagnostics mode

Set DIP 2 on and connect a USB-serial adapter's RX to J5 pin 4, GND to pin 3. Open the Serial Monitor at 115200. Every quarter second the board prints each stalk channel's ADC reading, the park switch state and what it decoded. Walk the stalk through every position; this is the stalk truth table without a multimeter. The Virtual bench tab prints the same lines.

S1=1023 S2=1023 S3=0 S4=1023 S5=1023 S6=96 S7=1023 S8=1023 | park=PARKED | INT(6.0s)

Before the board goes near the car, prove it on the bench with three 12V bulbs and a toggle switch standing in for the motor. Nothing on the board can tell the difference.

Build the test rig

Stand-inWire it
Power12V supply (3A+) or a battery through a 5A fuse → J1 pins 1–2; ground → J1 pins 3–4
Bulb A = LO brush+12V → bulb → J2 pin 4. Lit = motor running on LO.

Bulb C = pumpJ3 pin 1 → bulb → GND. Lit = washer on.
Toggle = auto-stopSPDT switch: common → J2 pin 2. One side to +12V ("parked"), other side to GND ("sweeping").
StalkThe real stalk through the harness, or jumper J4 pins 2–5 to J4 pin 9 (GND).

Start with the toggle on "parked". 5–10W bulbs keep the current low.

Run the checks

  1. Key on, stalk OFF. No clicks, all bulbs off.
  2. LO. K1 clicks, bulb A on. Flip the toggle back and forth: nothing changes.
  3. HI. Two clicks, bulb B on, A off.
  4. OFF with the toggle on "sweeping". Bulb A stays on until you flip to "parked". That's the blade finishing its sweep.
  5. INT. Bulb A on at once. Flip to "sweeping": K1 releases but A stays on. Flip to "parked": A off. Time the next flash at both ring ends: about 2 s and 20 s.
  6. Hold WASH. Bulb C on, A on after 0.3 s. Release, then toggle sweeping→parked three times: A stops after the third. DIP 1 on: two.
  7. Hold WASH for 12 s. Bulb C goes off at 10 s.
  8. LO with the toggle left on "parked". After 8 s all off, LED flashing fast. Stalk OFF clears it.
